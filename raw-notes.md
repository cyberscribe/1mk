# 1MK Rough Notes

One-Man Kanban: how to get the right things done and stay sane in the AI era

## The Name

Collaborating with AI has turned many of us into a "one-man band", marching along with a drum strapped to our chest, a tuba around our neck, and bells on our ankles. Without a score to follow, mental exhaustion can soon set in.

One-Man KANBAN takes the best bits of personal prodcutivity thinking, applies them to the format of a KANBAN board, and offers a set of rituals to manage it. The result is that you can write and follow your own score, marching confidently toward your goals as fast as possible but no faster.

The methodology empowers you to do this reliably and repeatably by introducing everything you need to orchestrate a healthy, happy, and highly effective coworking relationship with AI.

## Premise

The level at AI keeps track of work and the level at which you need to track work are *not the same*.

 - Humans need to track outcomes, because humans are existentially accountable to other humans for these outcomes.
 - AI tracks deliverables, because AI is accountable to you.

AI produces deliverables: documents, such as image, text sound, or video files; addditions and modifications to files in a codebase or database; transmissions that alter digital systems in some way such as an API call or automated email response.

Humans are accountable for outcomes. Deliverables are only one part, and AI is only one source of deliverables. You and your human colleagues make them too.

What is new is that AI can understand and act on requests for deliverables described using natural language. The world has been forever changed by a metaphorical interface, because it works.

More than this, AI can also help define and re-define an outcome as work goes on. In this way it becomes not only a fabricator but a thinking partner. 

However, the accountability to the outcome always ultimately rests with you. This is why, if you want to move fast and stay sane collaborating with AI, you need a system.

## GTD Provenance

The cognitive effects of AI co-working are not solved by GTD alone, nor are the benefits of AI co-working fully realized by GTD.

Some principles from GTD are sacred but implementation has not aged well; others are profane in the AI era.

Sacred from GTD:

 - outcome thinking about concrete deliverables
 - next step thinking

Outdated from GTD:

 - Numerous contexts for work; the pandemic killed this
   * e.g. the need for a "what can I do in 5 minutes in the back of a taxi?" calls list; look out the window, let your brain assimilate and relax
 - Unified One System to Rule Them All
   * work and personal should be a natural division; likewise any other areas you want to work on only at set times
 - Projects lists separate from Next Action lists with Next Actions reviewed daily and Projects weekly
 - Tickler files (use your calendar)

New in the AI era:

 - AI requires deliverable and next step correlation in order to be able to help you

## Loops

### Loops within Loops

**Open loops**, discovered by Blua Zeigarnik in 1920 and popularised by David Allen in 2001, are unfinished commitments that pull on your attention. Closing the loop means not only completing the task, but recognising its completion, freeing up psychological energy for other tasks.

**Agentic loops** follow a pattern in early robotics of taking input, applying reasoning to it, acting upon that reasoning, evaluating the results, and then deciding whether the task was accomplished, at which point it ends, or if not treating the evaluation as new input, starting the loop over again.

In both cases, recognition of completion is essential to closing or exiting the loop cleanly. Working with AI, then, involves agentic loops circulating within your own open loops. A single agent doesn't exit the loop until it is convinced the task is done. 

**Outcome loops** involve orchestrating numerous other loops along the way to completion, some involving your own work, some involving that of others, and some involving AI. The outcome loop then strings together these deliverable loops until the outcome is realised. Sometimes, along the way, the outcome or the path to achieve it must be adjusted based on new information. Ultimately, though, outcome loops are composed of deliverables generated by their own loops.

Humans manage many open outcome loops simultaneously, with AI loops playing only one part of the total contribution of deliverables along the way. We therefore need a way to close an open outcome loop without fully exiting it -- to pause work even though it is not recognised as done.

Your 1MK system for tracking outcomes and their current status allows you to pause and pick back up cleanly, which means you can free up psychological energy to attend other loops rather than fragmenting your attention across many loops at once.

### Cadence of loops

While individual agents loop until completion (including, it must be said, orchestrating their own loops-within-loops along the way toward a complex deliverable) humans must orchestrate loops across the lifecycle of numerous outcomes. Doing so at specific calendar cadences provides a framework that is easy to habituate.

#### The Daily Loop

**Daily Briefing**

An ideal daily loop starts with the **Daily Briefing** as its first input. This involves reviewing with AI:

 - Email and messages - for anything urgent
 - Calendar for the day - for time-based commitments and reminders
 - My...:
   * Upcoming - to move to In Progress
   * One-Offs - for quick wins
   * In Progress - to move if needed
   * Blocked - check for followup
 - Others...:
   * Discussion - reconcile against meetings
   * Waiting - check for followup
   * Delegated - reconcile against meetings

A single prompt should surface this, and allow you to act:

 - Urgent Email/Messages - flag quick ones, add to One-Offs or In Progress if longer
 - Calendar - rearrange if needed, note available non-meeting time, move reminders to In Progress as appropriate
 - Upcoming - move to In Progress as appropriate
 - One-Offs - move to In Progress as appropriate
 - Blocked - update State of Play and move to In Progress if followup is needed
 - Discussion - link to appropriate meetings
 - Waiting - add One-Off to follow up if needed
 - Delegated - link to appropriate meetings

**Raw Input Processing**

Review with AI your email and messaging systems for any commitments to add to your KANBAN board using the **Three Compoennts of an Outcome Card** or the format for a **State of Play Card**.

**Work**

Alternate between **Time-Based Work** such as meetings or other items on your calendar and **Orchestrated Work** between time-based work sesions, collaborating with AI and your colleagues.

**Orchestrated Work**

For each In Progress item that you can use an AI agent to progress, kick off that loop using appropriate context and prompting. Make note of the **state of play** in a simple document file, your own **working memory**. Use this to track state of play for as many agents running in parallel as appropriate, updating as it changes, including jotting down your own next steps.

You are now in the loop.

## Externalisation

 - "Mind dump" onto paper or simple text doc periodically
 - Notes while in meetings
 - State of play tracking for concurrent parallel agentic work

### Working Memory

Open text file, light markdown format suggested, to track immediate-term state of play activity states for both you and your AI agents; written to KANBAN before closed out

## The Three Components of an Outcome Card

 - Outcome statement - card title
 - State of play - comment
 - Likely sequence and acceptance criteria - description

### State of Play

At minimum, this is a text description of the very next step that will progress the outcome. If it is another human and the card is in the "blocked" column, name them first, and optionally add a "then.." followed by the likely next step once they complete their part.
